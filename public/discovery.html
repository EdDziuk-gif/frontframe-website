The Blueprint

Two working prototypes. Full of possibilities.

The Blueprint is not a questionnaire. It is not a generic template. It is a working prototype—a comprehensive exploration of what a website could include for a business like yours. Two different wireframes, each built for a different business structure, loaded with every element, every section, every feature that might matter to an operation like yours. Most of what you will see is probably new to you. That is intentional.

You are not looking at options to choose from. You are not making design decisions. You are looking at a mirror—a real, working example of what your business could look like in digital form. You see what is possible. You see what you had not considered. You see features and workflows that might solve problems you feel but have not named. Some of it fits your operation exactly. Some of it does not apply to you at all. Some ideas you never thought about become suddenly obvious once you see them in context.

Your job is simple: react to what you see. Tell us what does not fit. Show us what matters. Show us what you did not know you needed. That feedback becomes the specification for your actual site. You do not have to know how to plan a website. We give you the first draft. You show us what does not fit. We turn that into the final build.

The Problem

The friction usually isn't where it looks.

The owner is answering questions that should answer themselves. Scheduling depends on one person being available. Follow-up exists because someone remembers to do it. Administrative work keeps pulling expertise into tasks a system should be handling.

Discovery exists to identify where the friction is actually coming from. You see the Blueprint prototypes, react to what you see, and in that moment of seeing something real instead of planning in the abstract, clarity arrives. Before anything gets designed or built, the goal is to understand how work moves through your business — where decisions depend on specific people, what is limiting growth, and whether a website actually solves the problem.

Sometimes the answer is a website. Sometimes it's a workflow. Sometimes it's something else entirely. Occasionally the right answer is no FrontFrame project at all.

The objective is not to sell a build. The objective is to understand the system before changing it.

Fit

We sometimes say no.

Not every business should hire FrontFrame. Some situations require specialized legal, accounting, valuation, or industry-specific expertise. If that's the real gap, it should be addressed directly — not papered over with better software.

Some businesses simply aren't at a stage where FrontFrame can deliver meaningful value. Part of Discovery is making that determination before anyone spends money in the wrong place. A recommendation not to proceed is a successful outcome if it's the accurate one.

The Deposit

The $500 Discovery deposit.

Discovery requires a $500 deposit. If we move forward together, that amount is credited toward the project down payment. If I conclude that FrontFrame isn't the right fit — that the situation requires something I can't genuinely deliver — the deposit is refunded.

That decision belongs to FrontFrame, not to the client. Once Discovery proceeds and a written report is delivered, the deposit has been earned. A client who receives findings and decides not to act on them has still received the work.

Discovery is real work. The report is a deliverable. The deposit exists to ensure both sides take the evaluation seriously — and to keep the engagement honest on both ends.

Who It's For

Where FrontFrame fits.

FrontFrame works primarily with owner-operated service businesses outside the medical field. The common pattern isn't a shortage of expertise — most owners know their craft extremely well.

The problem is that expertise gets consumed by the wrong things: scheduling, intake, follow-up, administrative overhead, customer communication that should have been systematized long before it became a time sink.

The objective isn't to replace expertise. It's to stop wasting it.
