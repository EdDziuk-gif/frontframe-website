The Blueprint

A FrontFrame Blueprint says: here is how your business should work online.

Before FrontFrame builds anything, we produce a Blueprint — a document that maps your business in digital form. It covers homepage structure, service positioning, booking and intake flow, AI assistant role and scope, FAQ logic, customer filters, local positioning, and what you do after the site goes live.

The Blueprint is not a wireframe. It is not a list of pages. It is a thinking artifact — a clear, written picture of how your operation should work online, built from plain-language intake questions and refined through a focused conversation. Most business owners see things in a Blueprint they had not considered before. That is intentional. You do not have to know how to plan a website. The Blueprint is the first draft. You review it, push back on what does not fit, and the approved version becomes the build specification.

The Blueprint becomes the first version of the site. Nothing gets built without it.

A live proof-of-concept Blueprint is available at phoenix-grant.frontframe.co — developed by FrontFrame on its own initiative, at no cost, to demonstrate the methodology applied to a real public-sector intake problem. That example shows what a Blueprint looks like in practice.

The Problem

The friction usually isn't where it looks.

The owner is answering questions that should answer themselves. Scheduling depends on one person being available. Follow-up exists because someone remembers to do it. Administrative work keeps pulling expertise into tasks a system should be handling.

Discovery exists to identify where the friction is actually coming from. Before anything gets designed or built, the goal is to understand how work moves through your business — where decisions depend on specific people, what is limiting growth, and whether a website actually solves the problem.

Sometimes the answer is a website. Sometimes it's a workflow. Sometimes it's something else entirely. Occasionally the right answer is no FrontFrame project at all.

The objective is not to sell a build. The objective is to understand the system before changing it.

The Process

Four steps from first conversation to written findings.

01

Initial Conversation

A focused call to understand your business, how work comes in, where the friction is, and what you've already tried. No sales pitch. No intake form disguised as a discovery session.

02

Front-End Evaluation

FrontFrame reviews your current presence — website if you have one, booking setup, how intake reaches you — and assesses where the system is breaking down.

03

Prioritization

We identify what actually needs to change and in what order. Most businesses have more than one problem. Some are FrontFrame problems. Some are not.

04

Written Findings

A clear document that identifies the real constraint, describes what should be built, and outlines why. If we move forward, the Written Findings become the basis for your FrontFrame Blueprint.

Fit

We sometimes say no.

Not every business should hire FrontFrame. Some situations require specialized legal, accounting, valuation, or industry-specific expertise. If that's the real gap, it should be addressed directly — not papered over with better software.

Some businesses simply aren't at a stage where FrontFrame can deliver meaningful value. Part of Discovery is making that determination before anyone spends money in the wrong place. A recommendation not to proceed is a successful outcome if it's the accurate one.

The Deposit

The $500 Discovery deposit.

Discovery requires a $500 deposit. If we move forward together, that amount is credited toward the project down payment. If I conclude that FrontFrame isn't the right fit — that the situation requires something I can't genuinely deliver — the deposit is refunded.

That decision belongs to FrontFrame, not to the client. Once Discovery proceeds and a written report is delivered, the deposit has been earned. A client who receives findings and decides not to act on them has still received the work.

Outside Advisors

Sometimes we bring in an outside perspective.

Some discovery evaluations benefit from context FrontFrame does not have — an accountant who can verify numbers, a legal advisor who can assess a specific constraint, a domain specialist whose input matters before a build decision is made. FrontFrame draws on a small network of outside advisors when a situation warrants it. This is disclosed when it happens. It does not change the fee or the scope of what FrontFrame delivers.

Who It's For

Where FrontFrame fits.

FrontFrame works primarily with owner-operated service businesses outside the medical field. Pet sitters, house cleaners, personal trainers, photographers, bookkeepers, notaries, lawn care operators, massage therapists, handymen, and tutors are the core of the Standard tier. Consultants, coaches, fractional executives, and boutique agencies are the Professional tier. The common pattern across both is not a shortage of expertise — most owners know their craft extremely well.

The problem is that expertise gets consumed by the wrong things: scheduling, intake, follow-up, administrative overhead, customer communication that should have been systematized long before it became a time sink.

The objective isn't to replace expertise. It's to stop wasting it.
