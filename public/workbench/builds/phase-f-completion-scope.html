Phase F Completion Scope

Working scope for finishing Knowledge-Gap Resolution before FrontFrame steps back to define the full product roadmap. This page scopes the destination; it does not invent implementation increments in advance.

Phase FKGR completionWorking scope · 2026-09-06

Boundary

Phase F ends when KGR is a complete organizational learning loop. The system must be able to encounter something the organization does not know, prevent unsupported invention, develop possible resolutions with the appropriate human authority, promulgate the selected resolution, retain it, and use it thereafter.

Don't know → recognize the gap → develop candidate solutions → human determination → promulgate → retain → use next time

Phase F is not the completion of Standard or Professional client delivery. Those boundaries will be mapped after KGR is complete.

What is already established

  • Unsupported organizational knowledge can be recognized before ordinary SCR scoring and routed truthfully into gap resolution.
  • KGR has an active case/hypothesis/statement lifecycle and Candidate 2 Increment 4 is closed, deployed, and live-verified.
  • Management selection, sign-off, pruning of unadopted accepted alternatives, and publication are atomic.
  • Constitutional proposal development and Operator promulgation exist as a separate governed path.
  • Case #1 remains unsigned and unpublished and must not be adopted merely to advance testing.

Known missing capability

Human-originated alternative candidate

The current experience can leave the authorized human with a binary choice: promulgate the developed candidate or do not promulgate it. That is insufficient.

An authorized human must be able to propose a materially different candidate solution to the same gap and develop that alternative through the KGR process. The system's candidate is one possible solution, not a privileged answer that the human merely edits.

Reference case: an SLA candidate could be workable yet omit the owner's alternative that the SLA should depend on the client's selected platform. KGR must permit that alternative to become its own candidate and be developed on its own merits before human selection.

Completion behavior to prove

  • Recognition: unsupported organizational knowledge is withheld rather than invented and enters KGR.
  • Plural candidates: a gap can support more than one candidate resolution without treating alternatives as errors.
  • Human origination: authorized Management can introduce an alternative candidate, not merely approve, reject, or edit the model's candidate.
  • Development: each candidate can be refined, researched, tested, falsified, replaced, or accepted as the nature of the issue requires.
  • Selection: human authority determines which accepted alternative governs; the system does not choose organizational policy.
  • Promulgation: the selected resolution becomes authoritative organizational knowledge through the governed publication path.
  • Retention: provenance/history is preserved without creating a competing source of current authority.
  • Reuse: a later materially equivalent question or operating need can use the promulgated resolution rather than reopening the same knowledge gap.

Completion test

Phase F should not close because a numbered increment has been implemented. It closes when a real gap can travel through the entire loop, including a human-originated competing candidate, and the promulgated result is demonstrably available to the operating system afterward.

The exact remaining increments should be derived from observed failures against this completion behavior rather than specified prospectively.

Deliberately outside this scope

Do not use Phase F to settle the Standard/Professional commercial boundary. The working concepts are preserved for the post-KGR roadmap: Standard may culminate in an accepted Blueprint; Professional may begin directly with an established client whose public presence is already complete; and job-shop systems development begins beyond the Professional delivery boundary when a discrete solution can be expressed as a job contract.

Those are roadmap inputs, not Phase F implementation requirements.

Gate after Phase F

Architectural review → full FrontFrame roadmap. Once KGR is complete, step back and determine what client-delivery work actually remains. Define Standard delivery, Professional delivery, the Blueprint boundary, direct Professional entry, and the later job-shop model from the capabilities that then exist rather than from anticipated machinery.

The next post-KGR phase is provisionally Phase G. Its detailed scope should not be designed until Phase F closes.

Comprehension layer only. Canonical Markdown, accepted decisions, the Constitution, and verified build evidence remain authoritative.
